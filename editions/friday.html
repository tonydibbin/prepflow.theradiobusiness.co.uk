    The UK Youth Parliament is facing the prospect of being axed, a move that has sparked debate about youth engagement in politics.

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Britons are keeping their phones for longer than ever before upgrading.
    The average handset is now held onto for several years. The main reason cited isn't loyalty — it's the dread of moving everything across.
  • 2
    More people are writing 'done lists' instead of to-do lists to feel better about their day.
    Noting what you actually achieved, however small, apparently beats staring at everything you didn't. 'Got dressed' counts.
  • 3
    A study says the smell of fresh bread is one of the few things almost everyone finds comforting.
    Supermarkets have long known it — the in-store bakery is no accident. Few of us escape without an unplanned loaf.
  • 4
    Almost two thirds of us say we've cried at a podcast in the last year — grief, parenting and identity top the list of triggers.
    Researchers reckon it's the intimacy of audio that does it. The figure is even higher for under-30s, 71% of whom admit a recent podcast-induced tear.
  • 5
    The fastest-growing hobby of 2026 is hand-embroidery — sales of needles and thread up 180% in a year.
    Crafters say it's the perfect 'phone detox' — slow, tactile, satisfying. TikTok is full of 20-somethings showing off custom denim jackets.
  • 6
    Cycling to the shops is up, with more people using bikes for short local trips rather than big rides.
    Councils credit better cycle lanes. The number one cargo carried? A surprisingly heavy single bag for life.
  • 7
    Roses are out, peonies are in — UK florists report a 200% rise in peony orders for weddings this summer.
    Brides cite 'softer, less staged' aesthetics. Wholesale peony prices have crept up accordingly.
