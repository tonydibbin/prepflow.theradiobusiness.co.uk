04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Local 'repair cafés', where volunteers fix broken items for free, are spreading across the UK.
    Toasters, lamps and zips are the most common patients. Organisers say the biggest barrier is convincing people their old kettle is worth saving.
  • 2
    Pubs report a Tuesday boom — the new 'mid-week meet' is the fastest-growing dining occasion.
    Operators say WFH-ers like getting out the house mid-week without the weekend crowds. Tuesday wine sales are up almost 30%.
  • 3
    Almost 70% of UK households now have a smart speaker — but the most common use is still 'asking what time it is'.
    Researchers say we under-use the devices we own. Setting timers, playing music and weather queries make up two thirds of all commands.
  • 4
    Holiday firms report a 40% spike in 'shoulder season' bookings — September and October sun trips are way up on last year.
    Travellers blame school-holiday flight prices, unreliable summer weather, and 'August feels too crowded these days'. Spain, Greece and Portugal top the wish list.
  • 5
    Three quarters of dog owners now talk to their dog more than they talk to their partner.
    Top topics: 'what we're doing later', 'what we just saw out the window' and a surprising amount of Bake Off commentary. 12% admit they've apologised to the dog after a row with the human.
  • 6
    Tinned fish is unexpectedly trendy again, with younger shoppers leading the charge.
    Sardines and mackerel are being repackaged as a quick, cheap protein. Older shoppers are quietly baffled, having eaten it for decades.
  • 7
    A study finds the average person has a 'comfort route' they walk or drive even when there's a faster way.
    Familiarity, not speed, wins. We'd rather the known road with the nice tree than save three minutes on the ring road.
