04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Board game cafés are booming on UK high streets.
    Owners say the biggest sellers are the quick, daft games — not the four-hour epics. The most common cause of fallouts remains the rules of a certain word game.
  • 2
    A survey says most of us have a 'signature dish' we bring out whenever guests come round.
    Chilli, lasagne and a roast top the list. Very few of us, it turns out, have a second dish ready if the first goes wrong.
  • 3
    Community 'little libraries' — swap-a-book boxes on the street — are popping up in more neighbourhoods.
    The unwritten rule is take one, leave one. The most-swapped genre, organisers say, is the holiday thriller.
  • 4
    A study suggests humming or singing badly in the car genuinely lifts your mood.
    Researchers link it to deep breathing and a small dose of joy. Fellow drivers at the lights may not share the enthusiasm.
  • 5
    62% of us have lied about reading a book to look smart in front of someone we fancy.
    Most-faked titles: 1984, Pride and Prejudice and (awkwardly) the Bible. One in five admit to faking a whole dinner-party conversation about a novel they'd only seen the cover of.
  • 6
    More than half of UK workers would take a 10% pay cut to never go into the office again — but only 17% of bosses say they'd offer one.
    The gap is growing again after forced return-to-office rules. HR experts say the tension shows up in resignation rates, particularly for under-35s.
  • 7
    Half of UK adults can't name a single member of the cabinet — but 80% can name three Love Island contestants.
    Political engagement researchers say it's not apathy so much as 'noise overload'. People remember faces from things they actively watch.
