04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    The average Brit checks their phone 159 times a day — but says it 'doesn't feel like that much'.
    Researchers using passive monitoring apps say we badly underestimate our own usage. Most people guess between 30 and 70 times.
  • 2
    62% of us have lied about reading a book to look smart in front of someone we fancy.
    Most-faked titles: 1984, Pride and Prejudice and (awkwardly) the Bible. One in five admit to faking a whole dinner-party conversation about a novel they'd only seen the cover of.
  • 3
    Cycling to the shops is up, with more people using bikes for short local trips rather than big rides.
    Councils credit better cycle lanes. The number one cargo carried? A surprisingly heavy single bag for life.
  • 4
    Britons are keeping their phones for longer than ever before upgrading.
    The average handset is now held onto for several years. The main reason cited isn't loyalty — it's the dread of moving everything across.
  • 5
    The humble flask is having a comeback, with sales up sharply as people try to cut café spending.
    Retailers say the big winner is the 'keeps drinks hot for 12 hours' bottle — though most of us still drink it within the first one.
  • 6
    'Quiet' holidays are the new luxury, with travellers seeking places with nothing much to do.
    Tour operators report rising demand for remote cottages and slow trains. The number one requested feature: a reliable kettle and a view.
  • 7
    More households are growing herbs on the windowsill to cut grocery bills.
    Basil and mint are the favourites. Survival rates, gardeners admit, are 'mixed' — the basil rarely makes it past a fortnight.
