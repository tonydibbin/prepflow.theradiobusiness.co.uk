Today's top stories

  • 1
    Thousands of men in England with prostate cancer will now be offered a new, advanced radiotherapy treatment on the NHS.
    This innovative treatment can cut the number of radiotherapy sessions from twenty down to just five. It's hoped this will make a huge difference to patients' quality of life and ease pressure on the health service.
    Health · UK
  • 2
    A new report reveals that many households are struggling with soaring bill debt, but don't know that help is available.
    The spending watchdog says most people are unaware of special tariffs for things like water and broadband. This means many could be missing out on vital support to manage their finances.
    Cost of Living · UK
  • 3
    Charities are warning that thousands of 'hidden homeless' women are being missed from official figures across the UK.
    A recent charity-led census found that two-thirds of the women they counted would not have been recorded using the government's current method. This highlights a significant gap in understanding the true scale of homelessness among women.
    Social Affairs · UK
  • 4
    Illegal mini-marts that break the law will now face closure for up to a year, following a change in regulations.
    Previously, shops caught breaking the rules in England and Wales could only be shut down for a maximum of six months. This tougher stance aims to crack down on illicit trading and protect consumers.
    Law · UK
  • 5
    Police are warning that drug gangs are 'cuckooing' hundreds of homes every week, taking over properties to deal drugs.
    This practice sees vulnerable people's homes taken over by criminals, turning them into bases for illegal activities. It's a growing problem that leaves many victims feeling like prisoners in their own homes.
    Crime · UK

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A recent study suggests that listening to certain types of music while gardening can help plants grow faster.
    Researchers played classical and ambient tunes to a variety of plants, noticing a measurable difference in their growth rate compared to those in silence. So, next time you're tending to your petunias, perhaps put on some Bach!
  • 2
    The trend of 'micro-adventures' is gaining momentum across the UK, encouraging people to explore their local areas.
    Instead of grand holidays, many are opting for short, accessible escapades like overnight hikes in nearby parks or wild swims in local rivers, proving adventure can be found closer to home.
  • 3
    British pet owners are increasingly creating bespoke 'adventure playgrounds' for their indoor cats.
    From wall-mounted climbing shelves to elaborate tunnels and scratching posts, these feline-friendly installations aim to provide stimulation and exercise for house cats. Talk about purr-fect play!
  • 4
    A new appreciation for the simple joy of 'letter writing' is blossoming amongst younger generations.
    Eschewing instant messages, many are rediscovering the charm of handwritten notes and sending them to friends and family. There's something special about receiving a physical letter, isn't there?
  • 5
    Researchers have found that looking at photos of cute animals can improve concentration and productivity.
    So, if you're feeling a bit sluggish at work, a quick glance at some adorable puppies or kittens might be just what you need to refocus. Who knew procrastination could be productive?
  • 6
    The delightful art of 'guerilla gardening' is transforming neglected urban spaces into colourful oases.
    Community groups and individuals are secretly planting flowers and vegetables in disused plots and roadside verges, bringing a splash of green to concrete jungles. It’s a lovely way to brighten up the neighbourhood!
  • 7
    Many Brits are embracing the calming ritual of 'tea tasting', akin to wine appreciation but with a cuppa.
    From exploring different blends and origins to noting aroma and flavour profiles, enthusiasts are elevating their daily brew into a mindful and sensory experience. Fancy a proper brew?
