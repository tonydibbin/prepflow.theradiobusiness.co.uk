Today's top stories

  • 1
    A disgraced funeral director who failed to bury bodies and gave bereaved families the ashes of strangers has been jailed for 20 years.

    This prolonged dry spell is raising concerns about water resources and potential impacts on agriculture. Satellite images already show significant 'vegetation stress' across many areas.

    The Prime Minister has announced that regional mayors will receive a share of income tax, a move aimed at devolving power from Westminster.
    Andy Burnham says this will empower 'every postcode' across Britain, though critics are calling for more detail on how the plans will be implemented. It's part of a broader strategy to redistribute power.
    Politics · UK
  • 4
    A trial for puberty blockers can now begin recruiting children after a High Court judge rejected a legal challenge from campaigners.
    This decision allows the trial to proceed, despite legal action brought by groups aiming to prevent it. The trial's progress will be closely watched by all sides.
    Health · UK
  • 5
    Sainsbury's has agreed to sell Argos for £120 million, though the popular retailer will still operate within Sainsbury's stores.
    Under the deal, Argos will continue to sell Habitat products and offer Nectar points to customers. It marks a significant change for both well-known British brands.
    Business · UK

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    The simple pleasure of a good old-fashioned picnic is making a delightful comeback this summer.
    Families and friends are rediscovering the joy of packing a basket, a blanket, and heading outdoors for an al fresco meal, proving that sometimes the simplest ideas are the best.
  • 2
    A new trend sees people embracing 'book swaps' to refresh their reading piles without spending a penny.
    It's a wonderful way to discover new authors and genres, connecting communities over a shared love of stories, and giving much-loved books a new lease of life.
  • 3
    The satisfying ritual of sorting through old photographs is being hailed as a new mindful activity.
    Many are finding peace and nostalgia in reliving memories and organising their cherished prints, creating a lovely link to the past in our digital age.
  • 4
    There's a growing appreciation for the gentle art of 'letterboxing' – a treasure hunt combining orienteering, art, and puzzle-solving.
    Participants follow clues to find hidden boxes, often containing a unique stamp to mark their logbook, offering a fun, active way to explore the outdoors.
  • 5
    The comforting sound of rain on a tin roof is being celebrated as a surprisingly popular relaxation aid.
    Many are seeking out recordings or even just pausing to listen during a shower, finding its rhythmic patter incredibly soothing for unwinding after a busy day.
  • 6
    More people are discovering the quiet joy of 'nature journaling' – sketching and writing observations from the garden or a local park.
    It's a lovely way to slow down, notice the small wonders of the natural world, and engage creativity without needing to be a professional artist.
  • 7
    The classic game of 'hide and seek' is experiencing a resurgence, not just with children but among adults too, as a fun, active way to destress.
    Organised groups are even forming for playful games in parks and open spaces, proving that some childhood joys never truly fade.
