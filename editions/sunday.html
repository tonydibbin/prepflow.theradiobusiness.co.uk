04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Swimming in the sea and lakes — 'wild swimming' — keeps growing in popularity year-round.
    Enthusiasts swear by the cold-water buzz. Newcomers are advised to start gently, and that the gasp on entry is completely normal.
  • 2
    More than half of UK workers would take a 10% pay cut to never go into the office again — but only 17% of bosses say they'd offer one.
    The gap is growing again after forced return-to-office rules. HR experts say the tension shows up in resignation rates, particularly for under-35s.
  • 3
    Cash is making a small comeback — ATM withdrawals are up 14% on this time last year.
    Bank analysts credit budget-conscious households using cash to stay disciplined. The 'envelope method' is back trending on TikTok.
  • 4
    Half of UK adults can't name a single member of the cabinet — but 80% can name three Love Island contestants.
    Political engagement researchers say it's not apathy so much as 'noise overload'. People remember faces from things they actively watch.
  • 5
    A survey says most of us secretly prefer a night in with a quiz on the telly to a night out.
    Shouting answers at the screen remains the nation's favourite low-effort sport — even when we're completely wrong.
  • 6
    Almost two thirds of us say we've cried at a podcast in the last year — grief, parenting and identity top the list of triggers.
    Researchers reckon it's the intimacy of audio that does it. The figure is even higher for under-30s, 71% of whom admit a recent podcast-induced tear.
  • 7
    Brits are apparently buying more houseplants than ever — and naming them.
    A garden survey found nearly a third of plant owners have given their plants names, and a surprising number apologise to them when they forget to water.
