Today's top stories

  • 1
    Tributes are pouring in for Professor Jason Arday, the youngest black professor in the UK, who has died at the age of 38.

    Wildfires across England and Wales have prompted a national alert, with the Prime Minister confirming 19 homes have been destroyed.
    Thirty-seven fires are currently burning across the country, stretching emergency services. Military personnel are now being deployed to help tackle the blazes.

    After weeks of scorching temperatures, the heatwave is officially coming to an end, with a significant drop in temperatures expected this weekend.
    The fifth widespread heatwave of the year will give way to noticeably cooler weather. It's a welcome relief for many after record-breaking heat.

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Gardeners are finding a renewed joy in cultivating 'forgotten' heritage vegetable varieties.
    Many are discovering unique flavours and stories behind older types of beans, carrots, and tomatoes that have been less common in modern supermarkets. It's a lovely way to connect with gardening history.
  • 2
    The simple pleasure of 'window box gardening' is seeing a charming resurgence in urban areas.
    Even those without large gardens are transforming their windowsills into vibrant displays of flowers and herbs. It adds a lovely splash of green to bustling streets and brings a little nature closer to home.
  • 3
    A delightful trend for 'vintage picnic baskets' and traditional outdoor dining is sweeping the nation.
    People are embracing the nostalgia of proper wicker baskets, checked blankets, and even proper china for their al fresco meals. It's making picnics feel a little more special and less 'grab and go'.
  • 4
    New research suggests that 'solving jigsaw puzzles' can significantly boost cognitive function and reduce stress.
    It seems the quiet concentration and methodical sorting involved in piecing together a puzzle provides a wonderful mental workout and a calming escape from daily worries. Perfect for a rainy afternoon!
  • 5
    The delightful art of 'letter writing' and sending handwritten notes is experiencing a heartwarming revival.
    In a world of instant messages, receiving a thoughtfully penned letter feels incredibly personal and special. It’s a lovely way to show someone you truly care and value them.
  • 6
    Local libraries are becoming hubs for 'community skill-sharing workshops', beyond just books.
    From knitting circles to introductory coding classes, these spaces are fostering a wonderful sense of community and helping people learn new crafts and practical abilities from their neighbours.
  • 7
    The charming tradition of 'collecting decorative teacups and saucers' is making a comeback amongst home enthusiasts.
    Beyond just display, many are using these beautiful pieces for their daily brews, adding a touch of elegance and whimsy to their morning routine or afternoon cuppa.
