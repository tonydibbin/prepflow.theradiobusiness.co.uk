04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Half of UK adults can't name a single member of the cabinet — but 80% can name three Love Island contestants.
    Political engagement researchers say it's not apathy so much as 'noise overload'. People remember faces from things they actively watch.
  • 2
    A survey finds most of us have a drawer, shelf or cupboard we're slightly afraid to open.
    The 'everything drawer' is a near-universal feature of British homes. Inside: takeaway menus, dead batteries and one very important thing we'll never find again.
  • 3
    62% of us have lied about reading a book to look smart in front of someone we fancy.
    Most-faked titles: 1984, Pride and Prejudice and (awkwardly) the Bible. One in five admit to faking a whole dinner-party conversation about a novel they'd only seen the cover of.
  • 4
    The average Brit checks their phone 159 times a day — but says it 'doesn't feel like that much'.
    Researchers using passive monitoring apps say we badly underestimate our own usage. Most people guess between 30 and 70 times.
  • 5
    A study suggests we underestimate how much our neighbours would like to be asked for help.
    Researchers say a small favour often builds friendships faster than years of polite nodding over the fence.
  • 6
    The number of people learning to play an instrument as an adult is rising fast.
    Piano and guitar lead the way, with online lessons making it easier. The hardest part, learners say, is finding somewhere to practise without an audience.
  • 7
    Loneliness is now considered a bigger health risk than smoking 15 cigarettes a day, according to NHS England.
    Social prescribing — GPs referring patients to community activities — is rolling out nationwide. Walking groups, book clubs and choirs are reportedly oversubscribed.
