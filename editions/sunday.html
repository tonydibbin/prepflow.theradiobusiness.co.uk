04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Gardeners across the UK are discovering the calming effects of 'moon gardens', designed to be enjoyed after dusk.
    These special spaces feature plants with pale or silvery foliage and white flowers that glow in the moonlight, often chosen for their evening fragrance, too. It's a lovely way to unwind after a busy day.
  • 2
    A new trend sees people creating 'miniature libraries' inside disused telephone boxes, offering a charming local book exchange.
    These tiny, community-run spaces are popping up in villages and towns, providing a free and accessible way for neighbours to share books and foster a sense of local connection.
  • 3
    Researchers suggest that regularly 'doodling during meetings' can actually boost memory and concentration, rather than hinder it.
    Far from being a sign of distraction, scientists say that light doodling helps keep the mind engaged and prevents it from wandering completely, aiding information retention. So, get those pens out!
  • 4
    The simple pleasure of 'baking bread from scratch' is experiencing a surge in popularity as a mindful and rewarding hobby.
    Beyond the delicious aroma and taste, many are finding the process of kneading dough and watching it rise to be a surprisingly therapeutic and satisfying activity, connecting them to traditional skills.
  • 5
    Scientists have found that 'listening to the sound of rainfall' can significantly reduce stress and improve sleep quality.
    The consistent, non-threatening ambient noise of rain acts as a natural white noise, masking other sounds and promoting a sense of calm, making it a popular choice for relaxation. No wonder we Brits love a good downpour!
  • 6
    There's a delightful movement to 'rekindle the art of writing postcards' when on holiday, rather than just snapping photos.
    People are rediscovering the joy of sending a personalised message and a physical memento from their travels, bringing a smile to the faces of friends and family back home. Much more charming than an email!
  • 7
    More people are embracing 'upcycling old furniture' to give it a new lease of life, rather than buying new.
    This creative trend not only helps the environment by reducing waste but also allows individuals to create unique, bespoke pieces that reflect their personal style and add character to their homes.
