04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    The surprising power of 'mindful walking' is being embraced by city dwellers for boosting well-being.
    Taking time to truly observe your surroundings while walking, even in a busy urban area, helps reduce stress and enhances appreciation for everyday details. It's about noticing the small things you might usually rush past.
  • 2
    A growing number of people are discovering the simple joy of 'reading a physical map' for planning excursions.
    There's a real satisfaction in unfolding a map, tracing routes with your finger, and visualising your journey. It offers a different kind of engagement than digital navigation, often revealing hidden gems.
  • 3
    The delightful trend of 'retro board game nights' is making a comeback, bringing families and friends together for analogue fun.
    Dusting off classics like Cluedo, Monopoly, or even newer, more intricate board games provides a wonderful opportunity for face-to-face interaction and shared laughter away from screens.
  • 4
    Scientists have found that regularly 'looking at clouds' can surprisingly spark creativity and reduce mental fatigue.
    Cloud-gazing encourages a gentle form of daydreaming and pattern recognition, which can be a refreshing break for the mind. It's a simple, free way to give your brain a little holiday.
  • 5
    There's a lovely movement to 'rekindle the art of handwritten thank you notes' for expressing genuine gratitude.
    In an age of instant messages, a physical thank you note feels incredibly personal and thoughtful. It shows a little extra effort that recipients truly appreciate and remember.
  • 6
    New research highlights the emotional benefits of 'revisiting childhood playgrounds or parks' for a dose of nostalgia.
    Walking through familiar childhood haunts can trigger happy memories and a sense of connection to your past. It's a pleasant way to reconnect with simpler times and a younger you.
  • 7
    The simple act of 'watering houseplants' is being recognised as a meditative and calming daily ritual.
    Taking a few moments to tend to your indoor greenery offers a quiet, focused activity that helps clear the mind and provides a sense of accomplishment. Plus, happy plants make for a happier home!
