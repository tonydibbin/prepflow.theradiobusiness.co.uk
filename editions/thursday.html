04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A recent study suggests that listening to the gentle rumble of a washing machine can be surprisingly soothing.
    Apparently, the consistent, low-frequency hum of household appliances like tumble dryers or dishwashers can mimic white noise, helping some people relax or even drift off to sleep. Who knew chores could be so calming?
  • 2
    The delightful art of 'doodling' during meetings or phone calls might actually be a sign of increased focus, not distraction.
    Researchers found that people who doodle can recall more information from spoken presentations than those who don't. So, next time you see someone sketching, they might just be paying more attention!
  • 3
    The charming tradition of 'leaving out books for others to find' in public spaces is growing.
    Known as 'bookcrossing', this lovely movement encourages people to share stories by leaving books with notes for strangers to discover, read, and then pass on. It's like a treasure hunt for literature!
  • 4
    New findings indicate that 'gazing at the stars' can inspire feelings of wonder and perspective.
    Experts suggest that taking a moment to look up at the night sky helps put daily worries into perspective and fosters a sense of connection to something much bigger. All you need is a clear night and perhaps a cuppa!
  • 5
    The simple joy of 'building a den' is experiencing a heartwarming resurgence among families.
    Whether it's blankets over chairs indoors or branches in the garden, creating a cosy hideaway offers a fantastic chance for imaginative play and bonding away from screens. A bit of old-fashioned fun, eh?
  • 6
    A study found that 'listening to the rain' can have a profoundly calming effect on the human mind.
    The natural, rhythmic sound of raindrops is often used in relaxation apps, and now research backs up what many of us already feel – it’s wonderfully peaceful. Perfect for a quiet afternoon indoors.
  • 7
    The delightful pastime of 'collecting interesting pebbles' from beaches and riverbanks is becoming a popular hobby.
    People are finding joy in searching for unique shapes, colours, and textures. It’s a wonderful way to enjoy the outdoors, and you end up with a lovely, free collection of natural art!
