01 — News

Today's top stories

  • 1
    Initial findings from an investigation into a recent train derailment near Lewes have revealed a track defect at the site.
    The Rail Accident Investigation Branch is looking into the incident. An 'irregularity' was found, which could shed light on what caused the derailment.
    Transport · UK
  • 2
    UK inflation has reached its highest rate in four months, driven by a significant jump in energy bills.
    Economists had largely anticipated the 2.9% rise. Chancellor John Healey stated that the ongoing conflict in Iran continues to impact prices across the UK.
    Economy · UK
  • 3
    Thunderstorm warnings have been issued for several parts of the UK, with forecasters predicting potential flash flooding.
    The warnings will come into effect later today, Wednesday, covering northern England, eastern Scotland, and Northern Ireland. Residents in these areas should prepare for heavy downpours.
    Weather · UK
  • 4
    The Prime Minister has unveiled a new plan aimed at getting rough sleepers off the streets of England in time for Christmas.
    The initiative will offer housing to those sleeping rough, drawing comparisons to the 'Everyone In' scheme launched during the pandemic. It's hoped to provide much-needed support as winter approaches.
    Social Policy · UK
  • 5
    The consumer watchdog is investigating Trainline, Virgin Atlantic, and RED Driving School over concerns about 'drip pricing'.
    The Competition and Markets Authority wants to know if the companies are failing to show customers the full price upfront. This practice can lead to unexpected costs being added later in the booking process.
    Consumer · UK

What's coming up

    Who do you think deserves to win the NTA for best drama actress this year?
    Awards · Coming Up

    The winners of Love Island UK's twist-filled series have been crowned, following a dramatic final.

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A poll finds the nation can't agree on the correct way to make a cup of tea.
    Milk first or last remains a genuine source of household tension. The one thing we agree on: someone else should make it.
  • 2
    Slow cookers are flying off the shelves again as energy-conscious shoppers rediscover them.
    They use a fraction of the power of an oven over a day. The downside: the smell of dinner from 9am tests everyone's patience.
  • 3
    More people are switching their morning scroll for a morning walk, apps suggest.
    Step-counters report a small but steady rise in early-morning activity. The motivation for most? A dog that refuses to wait.
  • 4
    A study says the average person walks past their phone over 50 times a day without picking it up.
    Researchers reckon just seeing it nearby is enough reassurance. The phone, meanwhile, remains entirely unbothered.
  • 5
    A survey finds most of us have at least one app we've never opened since downloading it.
    The average phone reportedly carries dozens of unused apps. Top of the 'meant to use it' pile: language-learning and meditation.
  • 6
    Pub quizzes are reporting record team sign-ups, with niche rounds drawing the crowds.
    Music and picture rounds are the great levellers. The cleverest team name still wins more goodwill than the actual answers.
  • 7
    Allotment waiting lists have hit record highs across the UK.
    Councils in some areas now quote waits of several years. The most-grown first crop for beginners? Courgettes — closely followed by regret, as they produce far too many.
