01 — News

Today's top stories

  • 1
    Households across the UK are being urged to read their energy meters today as new, higher prices kick in.
    The energy regulator Ofgem has announced a 13% annual rise in household energy prices, with the latest price cap now in effect. Submitting a meter reading ensures you're billed accurately before the new rates apply.
    Cost of Living · UK
  • 2
    Teachers in England are set to receive a 3.5% pay rise, but unions warn it could stretch school budgets even further.
    Schools will need to fund some of the increase themselves, leading to concerns about the financial pressures on already tight budgets. This comes as the cost of living continues to impact many households.
    Education · UK
  • 3
    A new investigation has found major retailers are still advertising e-scooters for commuting, despite them being banned on UK roads.
    The Press Association investigation discovered e-scooters being promoted for 'commuting' and 'urban riding'. This highlights a disconnect between advertising and the current legal restrictions on their use.
    Transport · UK
  • 4
    Up to 150 former WHSmith High Street stores are set to close as a rescue deal for the retailer has been approved.
    A court has sanctioned a sweeping restructuring, which will also see steep rent cuts on most of the remaining stores. This marks a significant change for a familiar name on British high streets.
    Business · UK
  • 5
    The Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has accused Labour's Andy Burnham of inheriting a 'mess' in defence spending.
    Ms Badenoch criticised what she called a missing five billion pounds in the government's defence investment plan. This political debate centres on the financial future of the UK's defence capabilities.
    Politics · UK

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    Reusable coffee cups are finally outnumbering disposables in some city centres.
    Cafés offering a small discount have seen the biggest shift. The catch: most of us still leave the cup at home half the time.
  • 2
    More people are switching their morning scroll for a morning walk, apps suggest.
    Step-counters report a small but steady rise in early-morning activity. The motivation for most? A dog that refuses to wait.
  • 3
    Board game cafés are booming on UK high streets.
    Owners say the biggest sellers are the quick, daft games — not the four-hour epics. The most common cause of fallouts remains the rules of a certain word game.
  • 4
    The crossword and puzzle boom is still going strong, in print and on phones.
    Publishers report a surge in daily puzzlers. The most argued-over answer in households remains anything involving a cryptic clue.
  • 5
    Community 'little libraries' — swap-a-book boxes on the street — are popping up in more neighbourhoods.
    The unwritten rule is take one, leave one. The most-swapped genre, organisers say, is the holiday thriller.
  • 6
    Brits now spend an average of 14 hours a week on streaming — more than cooking, cleaning and exercising combined.
    The biggest jump is in the over-55s, who used to be the holdouts for live telly. 'Second-screening' means we pay less attention than ever.
  • 7
    A study says the average person walks past their phone over 50 times a day without picking it up.
    Researchers reckon just seeing it nearby is enough reassurance. The phone, meanwhile, remains entirely unbothered.
