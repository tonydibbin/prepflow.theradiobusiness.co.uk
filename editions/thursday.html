Today's top stories

  • 1
    The government is set to apologise to victims of forced adoptions in England, acknowledging the historic practice.
    Forced adoptions saw unmarried mothers pressured into giving up their babies, often facing immense emotional distress. The Education Secretary confirmed the apology will be issued on behalf of the state. It's a significant moment for those affected by these past policies.
    Social Policy · UK
  • 2
    Driving test wait times won't hit their target until autumn next year, much later than initially hoped.
    The Transport Secretary had aimed to reduce the backlog to just seven weeks by this autumn. However, it now looks like learner drivers will be waiting longer than expected. It's frustrating news for those keen to get on the road.
    Transport · UK
  • 3
    Good news for your wallet: inflation has unexpectedly remained steady, largely thanks to slower food price rises.
    The Office for National Statistics reported that higher petrol prices were balanced out by more modest increases in the cost of meat, dairy, and vegetables. This offers a bit of breathing room for household budgets. It's a positive sign amidst ongoing cost-of-living concerns.
    Economy · UK
  • 4
    A former health worker has been charged with trying to sell the Princess of Wales's medical records.
    The individual allegedly attempted to access her private information while she was undergoing abdominal surgery. This serious breach of privacy has sparked widespread concern. It raises questions about data security within the health service.
    Royal · UK
  • 5
    Tragically, one patient has died and three people were injured after an ambulance and a car collided in Glasgow.
    Police were called to the scene on Balmore Road on Tuesday evening, where the serious incident occurred. Investigations are now underway to understand exactly what happened. Our thoughts are with everyone affected.
    Emergency Services · UK

Quick hits

  • 1
    Research highlights a growing trend for 'digital decluttering' – organising digital files and photos.
    Many people are finding that tidying up their digital lives can reduce stress and improve productivity. It's like spring cleaning for your devices!
  • 2
    The humble 'picnic blanket' is getting a stylish makeover, with unique designs becoming popular.
    From retro patterns to waterproof wonders, families are investing in special blankets to make their outdoor dining experiences even more memorable.
  • 3
    A study suggests that 'communal cooking' – preparing meals with neighbours or friends – is boosting community spirit.
    Sharing recipes and kitchen duties is not only fun but also helps reduce food waste and fosters stronger local connections.
  • 4
    The simple pleasure of 'leaf art' is encouraging more people to explore nature creatively.
    Using fallen leaves to create intricate designs and collages is a free, calming activity for all ages, often shared on social media.
  • 5
    New research indicates that 'mindful walking' in urban parks can significantly improve mood and reduce anxiety.
    Taking time to notice the small details of nature, even in a city setting, offers a quick mental refresh.
  • 6
    The popularity of 'recycled craft workshops' is soaring, turning everyday items into unique treasures.
    People are enjoying transforming old jars, clothes, and cardboard into new decorative or practical items, promoting sustainability and creativity.
  • 7
    Experts are noting a rise in 'book-swapping clubs' as a sustainable way to enjoy new reads.
    These friendly groups allow members to exchange books they've finished for something new, fostering a love of reading without the cost or waste.
