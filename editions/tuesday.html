04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A new study suggests that 'hugging a tree' can actually reduce stress and improve your mood.
    Researchers found that the physical act of embracing a tree, even for a few moments, can release endorphins and create a sense of calm. It's a natural way to ground yourself.
  • 2
    The delightful art of 'making miniature gardens' in teacups or small pots is blooming across the country.
    People are creating tiny landscapes with moss, pebbles, and tiny plants, bringing a touch of whimsical nature indoors. It’s a lovely, calming hobby for any age.
  • 3
    Scientists have discovered that 'listening to birdsong' can significantly boost feelings of happiness and well-being.
    Even just a few minutes of natural birdsong can reduce anxiety and increase positive emotions, proving nature's soundtrack is incredibly powerful.
  • 4
    A charming trend sees people 'swapping homemade jams and preserves' with their neighbours and friends.
    It's a wonderful way to share seasonal flavours and build community spirit, ensuring no delicious fruit goes to waste.
  • 5
    New research indicates that 'spending time near water' – like a river, lake, or the sea – can improve mental clarity and reduce stress.
    The sound and sight of water have a naturally soothing effect, making waterside walks incredibly beneficial for our minds.
  • 6
    The humble 'picnic blanket' is seeing a resurgence as families embrace outdoor dining and relaxation.
    From local parks to garden spaces, people are rediscovering the simple joy of spreading out a blanket and enjoying a meal al fresco.
  • 7
    A fascinating study reveals that 'reading a physical book' before bed can lead to better sleep than looking at a screen.
    The absence of blue light and the tactile experience of a book help prepare the brain for rest, promoting a more peaceful night.
