04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A recent study suggests that the joy of 're-reading a favourite book' is more profound than we often realise.
    Researchers found that revisiting familiar stories can offer a unique form of comfort and even reduce stress, thanks to the brain's appreciation for predictable narratives. It's like a warm hug for your brain, apparently!
  • 2
    The surprising trend of 'adult colouring books' continues to provide a soothing escape for many.
    Originally thought to be a passing fad, the simple act of choosing colours and staying within the lines has proven to be a mindful activity, helping adults unwind after a busy day. Who knew crayons could be so therapeutic?
  • 3
    Experts are highlighting the calming benefits of 'watching fish in an aquarium'.
    It turns out the gentle, repetitive movement of fish swimming can lower blood pressure and heart rate, making an aquarium a surprisingly effective stress-buster. Perhaps a trip to the local aquatic shop is in order!
  • 4
    There's a growing appreciation for the simple joy of 'receiving a handwritten letter or card'.
    In our digital age, the tactile experience and personal effort of a physical message stand out, often creating a more memorable and cherished connection. It's a little piece of joy through the letterbox!
  • 5
    A new report suggests that 'the smell of freshly baked bread' can genuinely boost your mood.
    The comforting aroma is often linked to positive memories and feelings of home, acting as a natural pick-me-up. Perhaps it's time to dust off the bread maker, or just walk past a bakery!
  • 6
    The unexpected charm of 'collecting old coins or stamps' is captivating a new generation of hobbyists.
    Beyond their historical value, the thrill of the hunt and the detailed artistry of these tiny treasures are proving to be a surprisingly engaging pastime. Every piece tells a story, apparently!
  • 7
    Researchers are exploring the benefits of 'taking a scenic route' even for everyday journeys.
    Opting for a path with more greenery or interesting views, even if it adds a few minutes, can significantly improve mood and reduce feelings of monotony. It's about enjoying the journey, not just the destination.
