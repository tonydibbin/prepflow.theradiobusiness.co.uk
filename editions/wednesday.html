01 — News

Today's top stories

  • 1
    Nigel Farage is attempting to regain control of the political narrative, following recent questions about his party's finances.
    His stated aim for the upcoming by-election campaign is to position it as 'the people versus the establishment'. This follows his resignation as an MP to trigger the by-election, which he will stand in.
    Politics · UK
  • 2
    An inquiry is being urged to investigate alleged sex abuse and 'baby trading' within mother and baby homes across the UK.
    Thousands of women and girls, often in secretive institutions run by religious orders, passed through these homes from the 1920s to the 1990s. This comes as more details emerge about the experiences of those who were housed there.
    Social Affairs · UK
  • 3
    There's good news for those suffering from endometriosis, as two new tests are set to speed up diagnosis at GP surgeries.
    Experts hope these new tests will be a real game-changer, potentially cutting the incredibly long diagnosis waits patients currently face, which can be nine years or even longer. This could significantly improve quality of life for many.
    Health · UK
  • 4
    Some of the UK's biggest banks are reportedly failing their most vulnerable customers, by pushing them away from basic accounts.
    The financial regulator has accused banks of not adequately informing customers about their options. This raises concerns about access to essential banking services for those who need them most.
    Consumer · UK
  • 5
    Payouts for injuries involving e-bikes have now topped a staggering £110 million, leading to a rise in insurance premiums.
    It's a relatively new issue, with the first claim over a micromobility vehicle injury made just seven years ago. The rapid increase in claims highlights growing concerns around safety and regulation.
    Safety · UK

04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    A new trend sees people embracing 'mindful walking' as a way to connect with their surroundings.
    Instead of rushing, individuals are taking slower strolls, paying close attention to sights, sounds, and smells, reporting reduced stress and increased appreciation for everyday beauty.
  • 2
    Researchers are noting the growing popularity of 'community swap shops' for household items.
    These local initiatives allow neighbours to exchange pre-loved goods, from books to kitchenware, fostering a sense of community and promoting sustainability.
  • 3
    The enduring appeal of 'traditional board games' is bringing families and friends together for screen-free entertainment.
    From classic chess to intricate strategy games, people are rediscovering the joy of face-to-face competition and collaborative play.
  • 4
    An emerging trend sees people 'hand-making greeting cards' to add a personal touch to their messages.
    Instead of store-bought options, many are finding creative satisfaction in crafting unique cards for birthdays, anniversaries, and other special occasions.
  • 5
    A recent study suggests that listening to the gentle sound of 'rain on a windowpane' can be surprisingly calming for some.
    The rhythmic patter creates a soothing background noise, helping to relax the mind and even aid in falling asleep.
  • 6
    The trend of 'urban gardening on balconies' is gaining popularity, with city dwellers growing herbs and vegetables in small spaces.
    Even without a large garden, people are enjoying the satisfaction of cultivating their own produce, adding a touch of green to their urban environments.
  • 7
    Experts are noting a rise in popularity for 'vintage vinyl records' as a warm alternative to digital music.
    Many are seeking out classic albums on vinyl, appreciating the rich sound quality and the nostalgic experience of physically engaging with music.
