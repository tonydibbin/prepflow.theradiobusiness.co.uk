04 — Newsbrief

Quick hits

  • 1
    The surprising power of a good 'earworm' is being explored by researchers.
    Scientists suggest that catchy tunes stuck in our heads might actually boost memory and concentration for some tasks. It seems our brains are quite good at multitasking, even with a song playing on repeat!
  • 2
    A new trend is seeing people create 'miniature landscapes' in plant pots.
    Think tiny benches, little pathways, and even miniature ponds, all nestled amongst succulents or small ferns. It's a delightful way to bring a touch of the outdoors indoors on a small scale.
  • 3
    The simple pleasure of 'window shopping' is being rediscovered as a mindful activity.
    Psychologists note that observing shop displays without the pressure to buy can be a relaxing way to engage with creativity and trends, rather than just focusing on consumption.
  • 4
    Experts are suggesting that collecting 'interesting local facts' can deepen our connection to our communities.
    Learning about the history of a local landmark or a quirky detail about a street can make everyday walks much more engaging and personal.
  • 5
    The satisfying art of 'skip-watching' (observing what people put in skips) is gaining a cult following.
    It's a surprisingly popular pastime for some, offering a glimpse into the changing lives of neighbours and sometimes even a chance to spot a hidden treasure!
  • 6
    A study found that 'wearing mismatched socks' can subtly boost creativity and individuality.
    It's thought that the small act of breaking a convention can encourage a more playful and open mindset throughout the day. So, feel free to mix and match!
  • 7
    The delightful art of 'doodling while on the phone' is being recognised for its unexpected benefits.
    Research suggests that these casual sketches can actually help improve recall of information discussed during a conversation, keeping your mind engaged without distracting from the main topic.
